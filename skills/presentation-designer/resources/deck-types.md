# Deck Type Playbooks

Each deck type has a fixed set of constraints that override general defaults. Apply these IN ADDITION to the universal rules in the main skill.

---

## Deck Type 1: Investor / Pitch Deck

**Audience:** VCs, angel investors, accelerator panels
**Delivery:** Live (10–15 min) + left-behind PDF
**Ideal slide count:** 10–15 slides (Guy Kawasaki rule: 10 slides, 20 minutes, 30pt minimum)

### Required Slides (in order)

| # | Slide | Must Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | What does your company do in one sentence? |
| 2 | Problem | Who has this pain, how badly, and how often? |
| 3 | Solution | How do you solve it, specifically? |
| 4 | Why Now | What changed in the world that makes this the right time? |
| 5 | Market Size | TAM / SAM / SOM with sources (bottom-up preferred) |
| 6 | Product | Screenshot or demo GIF — show, don't describe |
| 7 | Business Model | How do you make money? Price, margin, unit economics |
| 8 | Traction | Metrics that prove demand: MRR, users, retention, LOIs |
| 9 | Go-to-Market | How do you acquire the first 100 / 1,000 / 10,000 customers? |
| 10 | Competition | 2×2 matrix or comparison table — position honestly |
| 11 | Team | Founders + key hires, with one credentialing fact each |
| 12 | Financials | 3-year projection (Revenue, COGS, Gross Margin, Burn, Runway) |
| 13 | The Ask | Raise amount, use of funds breakdown, milestones it buys |

**Optional appendix:** Customer quotes, technical architecture, full team bios, cap table

### Pitch Deck Rules

- Every metric must be sourced (if from a report) or labeled "(internal data)"
- No fabricated traction, no "conservative estimates" that are projections dressed as actuals
- Market size must be computed, not copy-pasted from a press release headline
- Team slide: the most relevant credential per person, not a full bio
- Competition slide: NEVER say "we have no competitors" — it signals poor research
- Ask slide: "We are raising \$X at \$Y valuation" OR "We are raising \$X on a SAFE/note" — be direct
- Font minimum: 24pt body; ideal 28–30pt (investors often read on mobile screenshots)

## Deck Type 2: Consulting / Client Report

**Audience:** C-suite, client project sponsors, operational leads
**Delivery:** In-person readout + leave-behind PDF
**Ideal slide count:** 15–40 slides (depends on scope; executive summary = 5 slides)

### Pyramid Principle (Mandatory)

Every slide and every section must follow the Pyramid Principle:
1. **Lead with the recommendation / conclusion** (the apex of the pyramid)
2. **Three supporting arguments** (the second level)
3. **Evidence and data** (the base)

Never bury the recommendation in slide 20. It belongs on slide 1 (or slide 3 after context-setting).

### Required Sections (in order)

| Section | Slides | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Executive Summary | 1–2 | Situation → Complication → Recommendation → Impact |
| Problem / Findings | 3–8 | Data-backed description of current state; MECE structured |
| Recommendation | 2–4 | What you recommend, why, and against what alternatives |
| Implementation Roadmap | 2–3 | Timeline, owners, milestones, quick wins vs. long-term |
| Financial Impact | 2–3 | Expected benefit, cost, payback period, risk-adjusted scenarios |
| Risks & Mitigations | 1–2 | Top 3–5 risks and how to mitigate each |
| Appendix | Variable | Supporting data, methodology, interview summaries |

### Consulting Deck Rules

- **Every data slide has a source** (bottom of slide, format: "Source: [Org], [Month Year]")
- **MECE test**: Each section's sub-points must be Mutually Exclusive (no overlaps) and Collectively Exhaustive (covers all the ground)
- **Horizontal logic test**: Read only the slide headlines in sequence. They must tell the full story without the body content.
- **Vertical logic test**: Each slide's body content must prove the headline of that slide.
- Data must be from verifiable, citable sources — no invented statistics
- All financial projections labeled "Illustrative" unless verified by client finance

## Deck Type 3: Executive Briefing (BLUF)

**Audience:** CEO, CFO, Board Members, C-suite decision makers
**Delivery:** 5–15 minute meeting slot (often asynchronous read)
**Ideal slide count:** 5–8 slides maximum

### BLUF Structure (Bottom Line Up Front)

| Slide | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Decision Needed**: What decision do you need from the audience, by when? |
| 2 | **Recommendation**: Your recommended option, in 3 bullets maximum |
| 3 | **Context**: The minimum context needed to understand why. 3–5 bullets. |
| 4 | **Options Considered**: 2–3 alternatives with pros/cons (table or 2-column) |
| 5 | **Impact & Risk**: What happens if they approve; what's the risk if they don't act |
| 6 | **Ask / Next Steps**: Specific asks, owners, dates |

### Executive Briefing Rules

- Slide 1 must state the decision and the deadline: "Decision needed by [date] to achieve [outcome]"
- **No analysis in the main deck** — executives decide; analysts analyze. Analysis goes in the appendix.
- No slide > 5 bullets, no bullet > 8 words
- The deck must stand alone as a document when read async (no "as I mentioned" references)
- If a CEO can't understand the recommendation from slide 1 alone, rewrite slide 1

## Deck Type 4: Product Demo

**Audience:** Prospects, customers, internal stakeholders evaluating the product
**Delivery:** Live screen-share or in-person demo (async video optional)
**Ideal slide count:** 10–20 slides

### Structure

| Phase | Slides | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 1–2 | The exact pain this demo will relieve — described in customer language |
| Product | 8–15 | Screenshots or live demo sections — one workflow per slide |
| Value proof | 2–3 | Before/after metric, time saved, cost avoided |
| Social proof | 1 | One customer logo grid or one specific case study metric |
| CTA | 1 | Clear next step (Trial, Book demo, Contact sales) |

### Product Demo Rules

- Every slide that shows the product must show the **actual product UI**, not a wireframe or mockup (unless clearly labeled "Coming Soon")
- Screenshots at 2× resolution; crop to the relevant area, highlight with an arrow or annotation
- Never show a slide with more than one UI screenshot — it fragments attention
- Demo flow must match the sales narrative — don't demo features the persona doesn't care about
- GIFs and embedded video clips are acceptable for complex workflows (≤ 30s, must autoplay on click)

## Deck Type 5: Keynote / Conference Talk

**Audience:** Industry audience, conference attendees (100–10,000 people)
**Delivery:** Live, projected at large scale, no leave-behind (separate PDF optional)
**Ideal slide count:** 1 slide per 60–90 seconds (30-min talk ≈ 20–30 slides)

### Structure

| Arc Stage | Slides | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 1–3 | Opening story, bold claim, or provocative question — no agenda yet |
| Setup (Status Quo) | 2–4 | What the world looks like today |
| Disruption | 2–4 | What's changing and why it matters |
| New World | 4–8 | Your idea, framework, or solution |
| Evidence | 3–5 | Data, case studies, or live demo |
| Call to Action | 1–2 | What you want the audience to do with this |

### Keynote Rules

- **Max 6 words per slide for primary slides** — the audience reads the screen while you talk; dense slides split their attention
- **Full-bleed images** are the default visual treatment — text overlaid on imagery, not slide-body text
- Body text minimum: **28pt** (audience is far from the screen)
- **Never read your slides aloud** — if the slide has everything you're saying, the audience doesn't need you
- **Never use bullet lists for the main narrative** — bullets are for reference decks, not storytelling
- Slide numbers are optional (usually hidden for keynotes)

## Deck Type 6: Internal Training Deck

**Audience:** Internal team members, new hires, cross-functional learners
**Delivery:** Facilitator-led (synchronous) or self-paced (async)
**Ideal slide count:** 30–60 slides for a 60–90 min session

### Structure

| Section | Slides | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | 2–3 | Learning objectives (max 3), agenda, who this is for |
| Module 1 | 8–12 | Concept → Example → Guided practice |
| Knowledge check | 1 | 2–3 multiple-choice questions (every 5–7 modules) |
| Module 2 | 8–12 | Repeat pattern |
| Summary | 2–3 | Key takeaways, "what to do Monday morning" |
| Resources | 1 | Further reading, internal links, contact for questions |

### Training Deck Rules

- **Learning objectives** on the opening slide (not goals — objectives are measurable: "By the end, you will be able to…")
- **One concept per slide** — same rule as always, but more strictly enforced (learners need time to process)
- **Examples must be real** — fabricated examples confuse learners who try to verify them
- **Knowledge checks** every 5–7 slides to maintain engagement
- **Facilitator notes** in speaker notes field for each slide (for consistency across trainers)
- Self-paced decks need more text on the slide body (the facilitator won't be there to explain)

## Deck Type 7: Sales Deck

**Audience:** Prospects (varies by seniority)
**Delivery:** Live sales meeting or async follow-up
**Ideal slide count:** 12–20 slides

### SNAP Framework (Jill Konrath)

| Letter | Means | Slides |
|---|---|---|
| **S** — Simple | Make the decision simple | Clear problem, clear solution, clear ask |
| **N** — iNvaluable | Show unique value | Why you, why now, why not a competitor |
| **A** — Aligned | Match their priorities | Personalize to their stated goals |
| **P** — Priority | Create urgency | Business impact of NOT deciding |

### Sales Deck Slides

| # | Slide | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | Personalized: "[Prospect Company] + [Your Company]" |
| 2 | Their situation | Show you understand their world (research-based, specific) |
| 3 | The problem | The cost of the status quo |
| 4 | Solution overview | One-paragraph description + product hero image |
| 5–8 | Key features/use cases | Each slide = one use case with screenshot + metric |
| 9 | Social proof | Logo grid or specific case study metric ("Company X saved \$2M") |
| 10 | Comparison | Where you win vs. competitors (honest table) |
| 11 | Pricing (optional) | Tiers or custom quote; never hide price if asked |
| 12 | ROI / Business impact | Calculator or scenario analysis |
| 13 | Implementation / onboarding | Reduce perceived risk |
| 14 | Next steps | Specific: "Let's schedule a technical deep-dive with your CTO" |

### Sales Deck Rules

- **Personalize slides 1 and 2** every time — show the prospect's logo, reference their stated challenges
- Social proof metrics must be verifiable (signed off by the case study customer)
- No fabricated ROI numbers; show methodology if you're projecting
- CTA must be a concrete, low-friction next step — not "feel free to reach out"
