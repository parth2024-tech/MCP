# Slide Type Library

Every slide belongs to exactly one archetype. Select by the message you need to land.

---

## 1. Title Slide

**Purpose:** Open the deck. Set brand tone. Establish credibility immediately.

**Elements:**
- Deck title (Display font, 40–52pt, max 8 words)
- Subtitle or descriptor (Body font, 20–24pt)
- Presenter name + title
- Date
- Company logo (bottom-right corner)
- Optional: Full-bleed background image at 20–30% opacity (brand texture or thematic photo)

**Rules:**
- No bullet points
- No agenda, no table of contents
- Logo is decorative — center the audience on the title, not the brand

---

## 2. Agenda / Table of Contents

**Purpose:** Orient the audience. Signal the arc so they can mentally follow along.

**Layout patterns:**
- **Linear list** (3–5 sections): Single column, each section numbered
- **Icon grid** (4–6 sections): 2×2 or 2×3 grid, each cell = section icon + label + 1-line descriptor
- **Timeline bar** (3–7 sessions): Horizontal bar with section markers, for conferences or all-day workshops

**Rules:**
- Agenda names **sections**, not individual slides
- Each section label ≤ 4 words
- Include estimated time per section for live workshops
- Current section should be visually highlighted when the agenda repeats as a section break

---

## 3. Section Divider

**Purpose:** Signal transition between major sections. Give the audience a beat to reset.

**Layout:**
- Full-bleed accent color or brand image background
- Section number (optional) + section title in white/inverted text
- Optional: 1-sentence preview of what this section covers

**Rules:**
- Never put content on a section divider
- These slides are not counted in content pacing (don't count toward slide-per-minute ratio)

---

## 4. Statement / Impact Slide

**Purpose:** Land a bold claim. Force the audience to stop and absorb one thing.

**Layout:**
- One sentence or short paragraph, centered, 36–48pt, high contrast
- Optional background: full-bleed image at 40–60% opacity

**Rules:**
- No bullet points
- No chart
- Maximum 25 words on the entire slide
- Use sparingly — maximum 2–3 per deck

---

## 5. Single-Point Content Slide (Standard)

**Purpose:** Convey one insight supported by ≤ 5 evidence bullets or a single visual.

**Layout:**
- Insight headline (top, full width, 28–34pt)
- Body: bullet list (≤5 items, ≤2 lines each) OR a single supporting visual

**Rules:**
- Bullets are supporting evidence, not the headline's repetition
- Every bullet should add a distinct supporting fact
- Never combine a bullet list AND a chart on the same slide

---

## 6. Two-Column Slide

**Purpose:** Side-by-side comparison, before/after, problem/solution, or two independent supporting points.

**Layout:**
- Shared headline (top)
- Left column: label + content
- Right column: label + content
- Optional: dividing line or whitespace gap

**Rules:**
- Columns must be semantically parallel (e.g., both are evidence, or both are options)
- Never use two columns just to fit more content — split into two slides instead

---

## 7. Three-Column Slide

**Purpose:** Three parallel options, pillars, or steps.

**Layout:**
- Headline (top)
- Three equal-width columns, each with: icon (top) + short label + 2–3 line descriptor

**Rules:**
- Maximum 30 words per column
- Use only when the three items are truly equivalent in weight
- Three-column works well for: product tiers, strategic pillars, methodology steps

---

## 8. Full-Bleed Image / Photo Slide

**Purpose:** Evoke emotion, establish context, or deliver a quote with high visual impact.

**Layout:**
- Full-bleed image (edge to edge)
- Optional text overlay: dark overlay strip at bottom-third, white text

**Rules:**
- Image must be ≥ 1920×1080px
- Never use stock photos with recognizable stock photo aesthetics (forced smiles, clip art poses)
- Text on overlay must meet 4.5:1 contrast ratio

---

## 9. Quote Slide

**Purpose:** Introduce a powerful external voice (customer, expert, famous source) to reinforce a point.

**Layout:**
- Large quotation mark (decorative, accent color)
- Quote text (italic, 24–32pt)
- Attribution: Name, Title, Company (12–14pt, lighter weight)

**Rules:**
- Quote must be accurate and sourced — no paraphrasing presented as a direct quote
- ≤ 50 words. If longer, edit to the key sentence.

---

## 10. Data / Chart Slide

**Purpose:** Show quantitative evidence for a claim. The chart IS the slide.

**Layout:**
- Insight headline (the conclusion from the data, not "Here is our sales chart")
- Chart (dominant, takes 65–75% of slide area)
- Source citation (footnote, 10pt)
- Optional: callout annotation pointing to the key bar/line

**Rules:**
- Headline states the takeaway; chart provides the proof
- Choose chart type from the Data Visualization decision table in SKILL.md §4
- Remove all chartjunk: no 3D, no drop shadows, no redundant gridlines, no double legends
- Only one insight annotated per chart — if two insights exist, make two slides

---

## 11. Diagram / Process Slide

**Purpose:** Show a process, flow, system, or relationship visually.

**Layout:**
- Insight headline (states what the diagram proves, not just "Our Process")
- Diagram (takes 65–75% of slide area)
- Optional: numbered legend if diagram nodes need detailed explanation

**Rules:**
- Choose diagram type from the Diagram Decision Table in SKILL.md §4
- See `resources/diagram-guide.md` for construction rules
- Every node and connector must be labeled
- Max 7±2 nodes; anything more complex goes in the appendix

---

## 12. Timeline / Roadmap Slide

**Purpose:** Show chronological sequence, milestone progression, or project schedule.

**Layouts:**
- **Horizontal timeline**: Best for ≤ 7 milestones, left-to-right chronological
- **Roadmap bar**: Quarters on X-axis, swim lanes for multiple workstreams
- **Milestone list**: Vertical list with date callout, for detailed project status

**Rules:**
- Milestone labels ≤ 5 words
- Indicate "past", "present", "future" with color or fill style
- Completed milestones: filled; upcoming: outlined; current: highlighted

---

## 13. Comparison / Options Matrix

**Purpose:** Help the audience choose between options by evaluating them against criteria.

**Layout:**
- Rows = criteria; Columns = options (or vice versa)
- Header row and column are visually distinct (background fill)
- Cells: text rating, checkmark/X, score (1–5), or colored dot

**Rules:**
- Highlight the recommended option with accent color column header
- ≤ 5 options, ≤ 8 criteria
- If no recommendation is being made, use neutral colors

---

## 14. Team Slide

**Purpose:** Introduce the team and establish credibility.

**Layout:**
- 2–4 people per row (headshot + name + title + 1-line credential)
- Headshots: consistent size, crop style (circular or squared with radius), and background

**Rules:**
- All headshots same crop, same background (or remove background consistently)
- One credential per person — the most relevant one to this audience
- Never include LinkedIn handles on projected slides (too small to read)

---

## 15. Social Proof / Testimonial Slide

**Purpose:** Build credibility with real external validation.

**Layout A — Logo grid:** 2×3 or 3×3 grid of client/partner logos (if the logos are the message)
**Layout B — Case study tile:** 2–3 tiles each with: metric + short context + company logo

**Rules:**
- Logos must be on brand-approved list — never use logos without permission
- No fabricated testimonials, no invented case study numbers
- Metrics must be sourced and verifiable

---

## 16. Pricing / Tier Slide

**Purpose:** Present product or service tiers for commercial decks.

**Layout:**
- 2–4 columns (tiers)
- Row 1: Tier name
- Row 2: Price (prominent, 32–40pt)
- Row 3: Feature list (checkmarks)
- Recommended tier: visually elevated (border, header fill, "Most Popular" badge)

**Rules:**
- Never show pricing without a clear anchor (what the price is per: seat, month, year)
- Highlight value of recommended tier without disparaging others

---

## 17. Problem / Opportunity Slide

**Purpose:** Establish the pain point or opportunity that motivates everything that follows.

**Layout:**
- Headline: states the problem as a fact ("70% of sales meetings end without a clear next step")
- Supporting element: statistic tile, or 2–3 pain-point bullets with icons
- Optional: empathy statement ("Your team feels this every week")

**Rules:**
- Problem must be real, specific, and felt by the audience
- Never fabricate statistics
- Don't dwell — one slide; then move to the solution

---

## 18. Key Metrics / KPI Dashboard Slide

**Purpose:** Show multiple KPIs at a glance (for business reviews, investor updates).

**Layout:**
- 4–6 KPI tiles in a grid (2×2, 2×3)
- Each tile: metric label (small) + value (large, 40–56pt) + optional trend arrow + optional sparkline

**Rules:**
- Label the time period clearly on the slide or in footnote
- Green/red semantic colors only for directional change (up = good, down = bad), and only when that is universally true for all metrics shown
- If a metric is bad news, show it honestly — never hide in data slides

---

## 19. Appendix Slide

**Purpose:** Provide backup detail for questions without cluttering the main narrative.

**Layout:**
- Label first appendix slide clearly: "Appendix" (section divider style)
- Each appendix slide has an index number: "A1, A2…"

**Rules:**
- Appendix slides are never counted in the main deck pacing
- Reference appendix from the main deck: add footnote "See A3" on the relevant slide
- Appendix follows the same visual system as the main deck

---

## 20. Closing / CTA Slide

**Purpose:** The last thing the audience sees and remembers. Must be actionable.

**Layout:**
- Headline: the single most important thing you want them to do or remember
- Optional: 2–3 key takeaways (bulleted, ≤ 8 words each)
- CTA element: contact info, next step button (for digital decks), QR code, or date of next meeting
- Company logo + branding

**Rules:**
- Never use "Thank You" or "Any Questions?" as the closing slide headline
- The closing slide must be a CTA — close with what you want, not a pleasantry
- This slide is often left on screen during Q&A — it must work as a standalone information artifact
